Nash-Williams–Tutte tree-packing theorem · the necessity direction

The Contraction
Camera

Pull the camera back until clusters of vertices blur into single dots. Everything inside a cluster disappears; only the edges between clusters survive. Do this to a graph that already carries k edge-disjoint spanning trees and something is forced: each tree still has to hold the r blurred dots together, so it must keep at least r−1 of its own edges. The trees share no edges, so the bill adds up: at least k(r−1) edges must cross. That inequality is not a technicality bolted onto the theorem — it is the receipt the trees leave behind when you zoom out.

what a packing is

Each tree owns n−1 edges and no edge is owned twice, so a packing needs edges just to exist. That is the partition condition at its coarsest setting — every vertex alone in its own part. The rest of this page is about all the settings in between.

3

Why r−1 is forced: watch one tree weld the blobs

Take a single colour and feed its surviving edges into the quotient one at a time. Some edges weld two separate pieces into one; the rest land inside a piece that is already joined and change nothing. Starting from r loose pieces and finishing at 1 takes exactly r−1 welds — so the tree cannot possibly have kept fewer than r−1 edges. It may well have kept more, and those extra edges are the visible reason a tree's image need not be a tree.

A part that falls apart is a wasted part

Build a blob whose vertices are not joined to each other inside G — the octahedron below has three such antipodal pairs. Nothing about the cross count changes when you split that blob into its components: there were no edges inside it to lose. But r goes up, so the toll k(r−1) goes up. The refined partition is a strictly harder test at no cost. That is why it is enough to check partitions whose parts induce connected subgraphs.

Reading the receipt backwards

Necessity is only half a theorem, but it is the useful half for saying no. Three copies of K₅ joined in a triangle by one bridge each: 15 vertices, 33 edges, densely connected inside. Contract each island. Three supernodes, three surviving edges — but two edge-disjoint spanning trees would owe 2·(3−1) = 4. There is no packing to load here, and the ledger tells you why.

What this page proved, and what it did not

Honest accounting of the accounting.

proved here, in full

Contract, observe that each tree's image is a connected spanning submultigraph of , count , sum over the disjoint trees. Every number you saw was computed from the graph: the tree check is union–find, the quotient is rebuilt from the vertex→part map on every drag, the per-colour survivor counts come from replaying those edges through a second union–find on the supernodes.

NOT proved here

This is the hard half — Nash-Williams' and Tutte's actual contribution, and the miracle of the theorem: the obvious price is the only price. The standard proofs go by matroid union (the union of copies of the graphic matroid has rank exactly when the condition holds) or by an augmenting-path exchange argument. Nothing on this page establishes it, and no amount of dragging will.

Three things it is easy to get wrong

  • A tree's image need not be a tree. is connected and spanning on supernodes, nothing more. It can carry cycles, and it can carry parallel edges. That is precisely why the bound reads and not . Section 3 shows a colour paying 5 where 2 was owed.
  • Parallel edges are counted, never merged. G may be simple; almost never is. Two distinct edges joining the same two parts contribute 2 to . Merging them would understate the cross count and break the theorem — which is also why the statement is about multigraphs.
  • Loops are deleted, not counted. An edge inside one part contributes nothing at this resolution. This is not a convention chosen for convenience: a loop can never join two parts, so it can never help any tree pay its .
the one sentence to keep The partition condition is not an inequality someone invented to characterise packability — it is the shadow k disjoint spanning trees cast when you defocus the graph. Necessity says the shadow is always there. The theorem says the shadow is the whole story.
