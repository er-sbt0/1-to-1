Product vs. Box Topology

A product of compact spaces is compact — Tychonoff’s theorem — but only for the product topology. Switch to the box topology and compactness collapses. The whole story is one word: finitely. Build a basic open set below and watch why.

The one difference that changes everything

A point of the product is an infinite tuple . A basic open set is a product of open pieces , one constraint per coordinate. The two topologies disagree on how many coordinates you are allowed to constrain:

Product topology ✅ Tychonoff
Box topology ❌ Tychonoff fails

Product topology = you may only pin down finitely many coordinates at once. That finiteness is exactly the hypothesis a finite-subcover argument needs — so Tychonoff goes through. The box topology drops it, and the theorem dies.

Why finiteness matters

A basic product-open set fixes only finitely many coordinates, so it always contains an entire cylinder: infinitely many coordinates are still free to be anything. Cylinders are “fat.” A handful of fat cylinders can blanket the whole space — which is what a finite subcover needs.

The punch line

Product topology. A basic open set pins only finitely many coordinates, so it always contains a whole cylinder of points. Open sets are unavoidably fat, points can never be isolated, and finitely many cylinders cover everything. Tychonoff: an arbitrary product of compact spaces is compact.

Box topology. A basic open set may pin every coordinate at once. In the single box is a lone point: the space becomes discrete. The cover by singletons is open and has no finite subcover, so an infinite discrete space is not compact. Tychonoff fails.

One word decides it: finitely. Allowing infinitely many constraints is exactly what breaks the finite-subcover machine.
